# PairEval domain model

The system uses one verified Google email as a person's identity. Platform administrators are configured through `ADMIN_EMAILS`; instructor eligibility comes only from `INSTRUCTOR_EMAILS`. A Classroom stores its assigned instructor emails; a Student row is a classroom membership keyed by `(classroom_id, email)`. The same person may have Student rows in several classrooms. CSV import creates pending memberships; `GET /me` records `activated_at` for all matching memberships on first verified sign-in. An assigned instructor may remove a membership while no evaluation exists in that classroom; removal is permanent and takes that member’s notifications and group-move audit rows with it. No snapshot is kept, because no evaluation evidence can exist at that point. The emptied group rows stay, and an emptied roster accepts a fresh CSV import.

## Evaluation lifecycle

1. An assigned instructor creates an Assignment with Group and/or Individual sections. Each active section has a future deadline, a work maximum, and criteria whose weights sum to 100%. The Assignment also carries its scoring and pairing policy: the score band (`score_floor`, `score_ceiling`), the `completion_threshold` behind the participation multiplier, the coverage target, the two workload ceilings, `min_comparisons`, and an optional `pairing_seed`.
2. An Assignment moves through `DRAFT`, `PUBLISHED`, `FINALIZED` and `ARCHIVED`; `OPEN` and `CLOSED` are not stored but derived from each section's deadline. Only a `DRAFT` with no active pairs may be edited. Preview calculates pair allocations without writing, and `GET /assignments/{id}/feasibility` reports the coverage and workload a publish would use, including why a target was reduced. Publication requires at least three nonempty groups, saves all eligible pairs, and sets `published_at` and `PUBLISHED` in one transaction. An Individual section assigns pairs only within groups of at least three members, where coverage is `m - 2` unless the individual workload ceiling reduces it.
3. Unpublishing returns an Assignment to `DRAFT` and requires a reason. It stores every pair and answer on an `assignment_events` row and then removes them, so the criteria become editable without the collected evidence leaving the database. Finalizing settles both sections and snapshots the formula settings together with the computed report, so a later formula change cannot rewrite a settled result; reopening it also requires a reason. Every lifecycle action is recorded with its actor, time and reason.
4. A Pair identifies one criterion, two subjects, and one evaluator. A student evaluator is in neither subject group for Group pairs, or is neither subject student for Individual pairs and belongs to their group. A targeted instructor Pair names an assigned instructor email. Coverage comes from the feasibility solver rather than a constant: for Group pairs it is the largest coverage the classroom can sustain up to the Assignment's target, bounded by the eligible pool and the group workload ceiling; for Individual pairs it is `m - 2`, meaning every member compares every pair that does not contain them, unless the individual workload ceiling reduces it. A Student has at most one active assignment for that pair, and superseded history can remain for audit without adding a vote. Publication remains possible when the target cannot be reached, and the reduction is reported with the numbers that caused it. A Pair also stores `display_flipped`, the randomised left-right order it was shown in; a recorded choice is relative to that order, so every read of a choice must undo it first. Workloads are then evened out until no two evaluators differ by more than one pair, a step that moves assignments between evaluators and so never changes a pair's coverage. Finally every eligible Student evaluator receives at least one subject pair per criterion, which is the one step allowed to push a pair past the coverage target. Per-pair vote counts are therefore uneven by design; scoring averages each subject over its own effective votes, which changes the precision of an estimate rather than its expected value, and is strictly better than leaving an evaluator with nothing to open. In a classroom with far fewer pairs than students the two cannot both hold: ten, ten and two members give three pairs and six seats for twenty-two students, so even coverage would leave sixteen of them with nothing.
5. A student saves DraftChoice rows for their assigned pairs. Submit copies all current drafts for that section into an immutable Submission and SubmissionChoice snapshot. The highest Submission ID for that evaluator, Assignment, and section is the effective snapshot. A later partial snapshot replaces the earlier snapshot for scoring; old snapshots remain for history.
6. A group reassignment is accepted only while every published criterion deadline in that classroom is still open. It records a Reassignment, retains Pair rows that remain eligible, marks invalid rows with `superseded_at`, fills assignment coverage with replacement pairs, and writes Notifications. Superseded votes stay in history but do not count toward current scores. A read-only preview reports the same categories of effects before the move.

## Scores and visibility

Six choices give the left/right subjects these points: `1/0`, `.8/.2`, `.6/.4`, `.4/.6`, `.2/.8`, `0/1`. There is no tie: a midpoint invites evaluators to avoid deciding. The points apply to the subjects **as they were shown**, so `display_flipped` must be undone before they are credited. Instructor votes multiply both points and effective vote count by `instructor_weight`.

For each criterion, a subject's quality index is its weighted average points, in `[0, 1]`. That index is mapped onto the Assignment's score band, `score_floor + (score_ceiling - score_floor) × index`, and multiplied by the criterion's weight and its section work maximum. The band is per Assignment, so a score means the same thing whatever the cohort size; dividing by the sum of all subjects' averages instead would measure a subject's share of its cohort, which shrinks as the class grows. A subject with no votes is outside the band entirely: absent before the section is settled, zero afterward.

Participation is not a score component but a multiplier on the personal score: `min(1, (submitted ÷ assigned) ÷ completion_threshold)` over both sections together, so reaching the threshold keeps the whole score and there is no reward for exceeding it. Not evaluating peers says nothing about the quality of one's own work, so the two are kept apart: a member's multiplier never touches their group's work score. A student with nothing assigned keeps a multiplier of 1, because nothing was asked of them. Groups of fewer than three members have no Individual section to answer for. A personal total stays absent until every part of it has been scored, rather than reporting a partial product. Group work scores belong to a group; the report repeats that group's score in each member's row. Students can read only their own row. Instructors assigned to the Classroom can read the full report, criterion details, coverage, and exports.

Coverage is measured per criterion and unordered subject pair. `eligible_evaluators` counts distinct eligible Students; `target_evaluations` is the coverage the feasibility solver found achievable for that pair, bounded by `eligible_evaluators`; `assigned_evaluators` counts distinct active Student assignments; `votes` counts distinct Students with an effective submitted answer. `missing_to_target = max(0, target_evaluations - votes)` and `shortage_to_target = max(0, target_coverage - target_evaluations)` distinguish missing submissions from a classroom that cannot reach the configured coverage target. Re-submission changes the effective answer without adding another vote. `instructor_votes` counts submitted Instructor answers without weight; `effective_votes` sums Student votes at weight 1 and Instructor votes at `instructor_weight`; `left_points` and `right_points` sum their weighted pairwise points in canonical `left_id`/`right_id` order. Instructor votes affect score calculations but do not count toward Student coverage.

### Worked examples

- Group criterion `Quality` has weight 60%, Group work maximum 15, and the default band 0.60 to 1.00. One answer of "left slightly better" between groups A and B gives A an index of 0.6 and B 0.4. A's ratio is `0.60 + 0.40 × 0.6 = 0.84`, so A earns `0.84 × 0.60 × 15 = 7.56` and B earns `0.76 × 0.60 × 15 = 6.84`. Note they differ: the six-point scale has no answer that splits a pair evenly. Group C has no votes: its interim Quality score is absent, and zero once the section is settled.
- Individual criterion `Contribution` has weight 100% and Individual work maximum 12. A student vote strongly favours A over B; an instructor vote strongly favours B over A with `instructor_weight = 2`. A's index is `(1×1 + 0×2)/3 = 1/3` and B's is `2/3`, so with band 0.60 to 1.00 their ratios are `0.733` and `0.867`, giving work scores of 8.8 and 10.4. The same calculation serves Group and Individual subjects.
- A student assigned five Group pairs who submits three has a ratio of `3/5 = 0.6`; against the default threshold of 0.9 their multiplier is `0.667`, so they keep two thirds of their personal score. Four of five gives `0.8 ÷ 0.9 = 0.889`, still short; with only five pairs assigned, nothing below all five reaches the threshold, while a student assigned ten keeps everything at nine. Saving a draft without submitting counts as nothing submitted. A later submission with only two answers changes the ratio to `2/5`; the earlier snapshot remains in history but no longer affects scores.

## Ownership

An operator changes instructor eligibility through `INSTRUCTOR_EMAILS` in server configuration. A classroom instructor may invite only an email in that allowlist. An assigned, allowlisted instructor may manage that Classroom, and the last assigned instructor cannot be removed. Legacy InstructorApproval database records do not grant access. No instructor or student API may read another Classroom's roster, pairs, evaluations, or report without matching membership or instructor assignment.

## Deletion and retention

Deleting a Classroom removes its active roster, assignments, evaluation and audit rows in one transaction. The same transaction writes a `classroom_archives` snapshot containing those rows, the actor, deletion time and a `retain_until` date at least 366 days later. Archived data is kept outside the active classroom API and is available for operator audit through database access.
